# Managing a Global Remote Team as a New Team Lead

## Overview

You're in a situation where many new team leads fail because they focus on tracking tasks before understanding the system.

Right now, your biggest risk is not that people are underperforming. It's that you have no baseline for what good performance looks like in this team.

With a distributed team of 10 people, mixed seniority, and limited business context, spend the first 30–45 days building visibility instead of trying to tightly manage execution.

## What You Need to Know First

You need answers to four questions for every team member:

| Question | Why it matters |
|----------|---------------|
| What are they working on? | Activity |
| Why does it matter? | Business value |
| How do they measure progress? | Results |
| What blockers do they have? | Support needs |

Don't start with "Are they productive?"

Start with "How does work flow through this team?"

## Step 1: Run 1:1s with Every Engineer

Schedule 45–60 minute conversations.

### About projects
- What are your current responsibilities?
- Which systems do you own?
- What are the biggest risks in your area?
- What work is currently in progress?

### About team dynamics
- What is working well?
- What is slowing the team down?
- If you were team lead, what would you change first?

### About reporting
- How do you currently communicate progress?
- How do you estimate work?
- How often do estimates miss?

Take notes.

After 10 interviews you'll quickly discover:
- Who really owns critical systems
- Who understands business goals
- Who frequently finishes work
- Who sounds busy but cannot explain outcomes

## Step 2: Build a Simple Ownership Map

| Person | Area owned | Current work | Confidence |
|----------|----------|----------|----------|
| Alice | API platform | Payment migration | High |
| Bob | UI | Dashboard redesign | Medium |
| Carol | Infrastructure | Kubernetes upgrade | Low |

The "confidence" column is your confidence in understanding their work.

Focus first on low-confidence areas.

## Step 3: Stop Managing Hours, Start Managing Outcomes

### Bad
- Worked on API

### Good
- Completed authentication integration
- Reduced processing latency from 800ms to 300ms
- Finished 70% of migration

Measure deliverables, not activity.

## Step 4: Introduce Weekly Status Reporting

Ask each person to send:

```text
1. Completed this week
2. Working on next week
3. Risks / blockers
4. Help needed
```

Patterns emerge quickly:
- Consistent progress
- Repeated blockers
- Lack of clarity
- Missed commitments

## Step 5: Create Predictability Metrics

Track:

### Commitment vs Completion

Example:

```text
Promised: 5 tasks
Finished: 5 tasks
```

or

```text
Promised: 6 tasks
Finished: 2 tasks
```

After 4–6 weeks you'll know:
- Who estimates well
- Who overcommits
- Who needs support

## Step 6: Use Engineers' Explanations as a Signal

Can the engineer explain:
1. What they're building?
2. Why it's important?
3. What done looks like?

Strong engineers usually can.

If explanations are vague for multiple weeks, investigate:
- Unclear requirements
- Unclear project goals
- Technical debt
- Potential performance issues

## Step 7: Identify Senior Engineers Early

Look for people who:
- Unblock others
- Review many PRs
- Know multiple systems
- Understand business context
- Are trusted by teammates

Partner with them heavily during your first months.

## Step 8: Manage Mid-Level Engineers Effectively

Evaluate:
- Reliability
- Communication
- Learning velocity
- Ability to complete commitments

A mid-level engineer finishing 90% of commitments consistently is often more valuable than someone brilliant who finishes 40%.

## Practical Cadence for the First Month

### Every Week

**Monday**
- Team sync (30 min)
- Review priorities

**During the week**
- Individual 1:1s (30 min)

**Friday**
- Written status updates
- Review blockers

### Every Sprint

Review:
- Planned vs completed work
- Major incidents
- Technical risks
- Stakeholder feedback

### Every Month

Ask:
- What delivered business value?
- Who is overloaded?
- Where did we miss expectations and why?

## Key Mindset

As a new team lead, your job is not to determine whether people are working hard.

Your job is to make it impossible for lack of progress to stay invisible.

If every engineer can regularly show:
- Commitments
- Outcomes
- Blockers
- Ownership

Then both performance issues and high performance become obvious within a few weeks.

### Recommended Timeline

- First 30 days: Learn and build visibility
- Next 30 days: Improve predictability
- After that: Start making informed judgments about performance
